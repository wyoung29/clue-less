The Method

How a game unfolds

Clue-Less is played in turns. Movement is restricted — you can only enter a room through its connecting hallway, and no two suspects may share one. Every turn, the board tightens around the truth.

1

Gather the suspects

Three to six players join a lobby. Each is assigned a character — Scarlet, Mustard, White, Green, Peacock, or Plum — and receives a secret hand of cards.

2

Move through the manor

On your turn, move your token into an adjacent room or hallway. Secret passages between corner rooms offer a shortcut. You can also stay put if a suggestion is on the table.

3

Make a suggestion

Once inside a room, name a suspect and a weapon. Your opponents reveal a card from their hand to disprove you — or stay silent if they can't. Each answer narrows the case file.

4

Accuse and close the case

When you're certain, make your accusation: suspect, weapon, and room. A correct accusation ends the game immediately. A wrong one eliminates you from contention — but the case goes on.

Under the hood

Built for real-time play

Live over WebSocket

Every move, suggestion, and accusation is pushed instantly to all players. No polling, no refreshing — the board updates as it happens.

Private by design

Your hand is yours alone. Card reveals and accusation results are unicast directly to the right player — nobody else sees what you hold.

Turn order enforced

The server validates every action against the current turn and board state, so no one can move out of order or make an illegal suggestion.

Three to six players

Games require at least three players before the host can start. The lobby holds up to six, one for each classic suspect in the manor.
